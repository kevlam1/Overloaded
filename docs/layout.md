# The table screen: layout, and the plan for the grid battlefield

Written 2026-11-06, after Kevin asked to reorganize the layout of the table ("I need to scroll up and down to see people's battlefields", the Lands / Other permanents split is poorly organized, no page scrolling, and a coming way to play cards by drag and drop anywhere on a battlefield with the cards snapping to a grid). This is not a visual restyle; phones and tablets stay on hold.

## What Kevin decided (2026-11-06)
- The smallest screen the table must fit without scrolling: **1366x768** (a browser window of about 1366x650 on it) and up.
- Every player's board the **same size, in a 2x2 grid, yours bottom-left** (up to 4 players).
- A card played without dragging lands in the **first empty cell, lands apart** (lands fill from the bottom-left, everything else from the top-left); an Auto-arrange button tidies the board.
- Built in two steps, both on 2026-11-06: **Phase A** (the one-screen layout, with the old two rows kept), then **Phase B** (the grid battlefield and drag and drop).
- His earlier design notes (2026-10-25) said the battlefield should arrange itself, not free drag and drop. The new instruction replaces that; `docs/design-notes.md` is the history.

## Phase A: built (log entry 109)
- One grid on `main.table` while the game shows (the class `playing` is put on the body by the page; the solo table also gets `solo`). Rows: top bar, controls, the boards, your hand; one column on the right. `#game`, `#opponents` and `#opponent-list` are `display: contents` so everything in them is placed directly. **No element ID changed**, so the page scripts and the checks work as before.
- Boards: with 3 other players a 2x2 grid (yours bottom-left); 2 others: two on top and yours across the bottom; 1 other: one across the top, yours across the bottom; none: yours fills the middle. `main[data-opponents]` carries the count (set by `renderOpponents`).
- Cards are sized from the height of the row they are in (container query units), so they always fit; the old window-height sizes remain as the fallback. A row that is too full scrolls sideways inside itself; the page never scrolls.
- The right column: who is at the table with the match and sound buttons, then your counters, the selected card and your piles. Each scrolls inside itself.
- Each other player's panel: name and numbers on one line, their counters under it, their battlefield (the two rows; one grid since Phase B) beside a narrow column for the command zone, graveyard and exile.
- **Hover preview** (`frontend/src/card-hover.js`): the cards are small, so the pointer resting on any card shows its normal-size picture beside it (after 0.35 s; not on touch; hides on a click, a menu, Escape or a scroll). Your notes called this "enlarge on hover"; it is needed for the small cards.
- Checks: `scripts/check-table-layout.mjs` (5 window sizes x 0 to 3 other players: nothing scrolls, nothing overlaps or clips, the boards are the right grid, your hand runs under them, card sizes), `scripts/test-card-hover.mjs`, the hover block in `scripts/check-solo-ui.mjs`, and the live two-browser check run at 1366x650 (`--size=1366x650`).
- **Not checked:** a real four-player game (the layout check fills fake panels built like the page's own; the two-player table was looked at for real), how it looks on a monitor smaller than 1366 wide, and phones.

## Phase B: built (2026-11-06, log entry 110)
- **One grid per battlefield**, no Lands / Other split: **12 columns by 4 rows (48 cells)** for every player (`shared/board-grid.mjs`: `BOARD_COLS`, `BOARD_ROWS`, `BOARD_CAPACITY`). The numbers are mine, chosen because 12 x 4 gives a cell a little taller than wide at the size a board has on the 2x2 layout; they are one place in one file if Kevin wants another shape. Another player's board is the same grid, smaller, with each card on the cell its owner stored.
- **Where a card is stored:** `px` (column, 0 to 11) and `py` (row, 0 at the top) on the card, written when it arrives and when it is moved, so a card never jumps because another was placed. **No rules change:** the published rules already allow `px` and `py` from 0 to 4000. A card with no cell, or a cell off the grid or taken (an older game, a damaged value) is given the first empty cell when the board is drawn or the game is loaded (`settleBoard`, `placeAll`).
- **Auto-placement:** a card entering the battlefield (moved in, a token, a copy) takes the first empty cell for its kind: lands from the bottom-left (bottom row first), everything else from the top-left. **Auto-arrange** (button, solo and live table) re-places every card by that rule, lands and others each sorted by name so copies sit side by side.
- **Drag and drop** (`frontend/src/board-drag.js`; mouse and pen only, the touch version stays on hold): lift one of your cards from the battlefield or your hand, a picture follows the pointer and the cell under it is outlined, drop it and it snaps. A battlefield card dropped on another card **swaps places** with it (dashed outline); a hand card cannot take a taken cell ("That space is taken.", red outline). A drag starts only after 6 px, so a click still selects; a press on a counter chip or a badge is theirs; Escape, a menu, the window losing focus, or letting go outside the battlefield cancels it. A card from the hand dragged onto the grid is played there.
- **Without a mouse:** with a card on your battlefield selected, Alt and an arrow key move it one cell (swapping if the cell is taken); at the edge it says so.
- **A full board** (48 cards) refuses another card in words ("The battlefield is full (48 cards fit). Move a card off it first.") instead of stacking them. The database's own limit is 250 per list, so the grid is the tighter limit; nothing in the rules changed.
- **Cost of a drag:** a zone that holds the same cards in the same places as before is written one card at a time (`public/{uid}/battlefield/{i}`, about 100 bytes each); a drag or a swap writes one or two small pieces, a tap one. Any other change still writes the whole zone.
- **Game logic stays plain:** `shared/zones.mjs` (`moveCard` with a `cell`, `placeCard` with its swap, `arrangeBoard`, `settleBoard`, `boardCells`, `checkGame` checking cells) and `shared/game-sync.mjs`, tested in Node.
- **Checks:** `scripts/test-board-grid.mjs` (99), `scripts/test-board-play.mjs` (99, with five random games of 2,500 operations), the drag block in `scripts/check-solo-ui.mjs` (real mouse, touch and Alt-key input against the real page: drop on an empty cell, taken-cell refusal, swap, move, a 2 px click, Escape, drop outside, badge press, right button, a finger, Alt arrows, Auto-arrange), and the live two-browser check, which now also asserts that the other player sees the cell that was stored.
- **Measured, and worth a decision:** the grid makes cards small on the busiest layout. With three opponents a card is 26 px tall at 1366x650, 31 px at 1366x768 and 55 px at 1920x950 on another player's board (yours: 40 px at 1366x650, 37 at 1280x720), and the layout check holds them to at least 24 px for others and 36 px for yours. They are read with the hover preview. If that is too small, the knobs are fewer columns or rows (one constant each), or moving the counters, command zone, graveyard and exile off the opponent panel.
- **Not built:** a touch version of the drag, dragging a card onto another player's board, dragging between the battlefield and the command zone or piles (use the buttons or the menu), free placement that is not on the grid, a "stack" area, a pointer-follow zoom while dragging.
- **Not checked:** a real four-player game with real pictures; phones; the content policy on a deployed site for the drag picture's size (it is set from script on the element's style, which the page's policy allows for the CSSOM, but only a deployed run proves it).
